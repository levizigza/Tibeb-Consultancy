# Brand strategy

Status: Direction A (Clear ground) is the live design system. The owner requested a Latin wordmark on 10 October 2026. Name meaning, Ge'ez spelling, founder facts, and voice samples in the checklist are still open. Do not add those until the matching row is approved.

This strategy is for a later implementation pass. It does not publish a new logo, a founder story, or a claim that the name has been explained.

The only positioning sentence supplied for the project remains a draft:

> TIBEB Consultancy is a Black-owned, Ethiopian-led professional-services business in Canada.

Confirm that sentence before treating it as the public line. Do not extend it with history, a city, a year, or a definition of the name.

## 1. Strategy

TIBEB should feel competent, plain-spoken, culturally literate, and easy to approach. The audience is not one community. The business is Black-owned and Ethiopian-led, and it is for clients of any background who need a careful explanation of a Canadian process.

The brand has to carry two truths at once:

- The work is professional and specific. People should see scope, limits, and plain language before they see decoration.
- The culture is real. It should show up as language, care, and structure, not as a costume applied to a generic consultancy.

What the public brand may say, once the owner approves the words:

- The business is Black-owned and Ethiopian-led.
- It works in Canada.
- English is the language of the current site copy. Amharic, Tigrinya, and Afaan Oromoo are planned website languages. They are not evidence that consultations are offered in those languages.
- Client services are not open until a service passes the compliance and publication gates.

What the public brand must not say:

- A meaning of the name, unless the owner confirms it.
- A founder biography, credential, city of origin, faith, or years of experience.
- That the business is licensed, authorized, or affiliated with a government body.
- That a service can be booked.
- A promise of an immigration, tax, employment, or legal outcome.

Professional-services sites that earn trust share a few visible habits: a quiet page, one clear action, scope written in ordinary words, and proof that is real (a name, a licence, an address) rather than a mood. Sites that fail this category hide thin services under stock handshakes, gold crests, and slogans about journeys. TIBEB should follow the first habit. Proof that is not on file stays off the page. Empty space is more honest than a fake credential.

## 2. Cultural research

### What is worth learning

Contemporary Ethiopian graphic design treats the Ge'ez script as a design system, not a texture. Addis Ababa studios and type designers are building Ethiopic typefaces for brand and campaign work. The 2024 exhibition *Ethiopia at the Crossroads* used custom type based on Ge'ez for wayfinding. The useful lesson is that language can be the cultural mark. A pattern fill is not required.

Museum and textile records describe a second, specific tradition. Wiktionary, citing Kane (1990) and Leslau (2000), gives Amharic ጥበብ (*ṭəbäb*) as ability, skill, wisdom, and knowledge, and Tigrinya ጥበብ as art and wisdom. Collection notes from the Powerhouse Museum (Sydney) and the Museum of Anthropology at the University of British Columbia use *tibeb* for the coloured supplementary-weft border woven into Ethiopian and Eritrean shawls and dresses, and they connect that word to art or wisdom. A Textile Society paper, "TIBEB, Art of the Weaver in Addis Ababa Today," describes the same woven border, including Dorze weavers in Shiro Meda. Those sources do not say why this business chose the name.

No reliable Afaan Oromoo gloss was confirmed in this review. Do not publish an Oromo translation of the name.

### What to refuse

Authentic use is specific, credited, and limited. Imitation is generic, sacred, or borrowed from the wrong place.

Do not use:

- The Ethiopian flag, or a green-yellow-red stripe, as the logo. The proposed palette already avoids those colours. Keep it that way unless the owner asks for a documented reason.
- The Lion of Judah, Orthodox crosses, or church ornament as a business icon. Traditional tibeb often includes crosses. Copying them would assign the founder a faith that has not been stated, and it would narrow a practice that says it welcomes every background.
- West African patterns (kente, Ankara, adinkra) as a stand-in for an Ethiopian-led business. That is a different history.
- Coffee-ceremony clipart, Aksum stelae, or a generic "African sunset" as decoration.
- A model in traditional dress who is not a person the business has permission to show.

A single horizontal rule can suggest a woven edge later, if the owner confirms that the textile meaning is intended. Until then, a rule is only a layout line. It is not a tibeb pattern, and it should not be labelled as one.

## 4. Visual principles

1. **Ground, then edge.** Most of the page is ivory and ink. Colour is for action and for one accent, not for a pattern.
2. **Culture is linguistic.** Ethiopic script appears on approved Amharic and Tigrinya text. It is not sprinkled on English as ornament. Afaan Oromoo uses Latin type.
3. **Proof over mood.** If a fact is not approved, the layout does not invent a portrait, a crest, or a statistic to fill the gap.
4. **One dark accent that can carry a label.** Seal brown is the action colour. Gold is the metal edge beside that brown. Gold does not carry text on the parchment page.
5. **Colour is never the only signal.** Status, links, and errors need text or an icon as well as a hue.
6. **Motion stays quiet.** Keep the existing durations and the reduced-motion rule. Do not add a weaving animation.
7. **The same kit in both schemes.** Light is the primary brand. Dark is ink used as the page, not a second palette.

## 5. Three directions

Contrast below is for the proposed colours, calculated for WCAG. Body text needs 4.5:1. Large text and user-interface boundaries need 3:1.

| Ink on stone `#E7E3DB` | 12.48:1 | Text on a tinted panel |
| Teal `#17675E` on ivory | 6.10:1 | Links, or ivory text on a teal control |

| Gold on stone | 2.49:1 | Fails for text |
| Gold on ink | 5.01:1 | Short text on an ink ground only |
| Teal on ink | 2.39:1 | Do not put teal type or a teal focus ring on ink |

### Direction A — Clear ground

Ivory page, ink text, teal for links and the primary control, stone for a quiet panel. Gold is absent, or it is a non-text hairline that is never the only focus style. The wordmark is the Latin name in the text face. No pattern, no photograph, no symbol beyond type.

Strengths: matches a professional-services promise, meets contrast without workarounds, and can be built with the fonts already loaded. It does not pretend the founder has been photographed or that the name has been interpreted.

Drawbacks: a careless pass could look like any calm consultancy. The cultural position then depends on real bilingual type and on approved words, which do not exist yet.

Accessibility: the text pairs above pass AA. Focus is a 2px ink ring on ivory, or a 2px ivory ring on a teal control, with a 3px offset. Gold is not the focus colour.

Maintenance: low. Token swap in `globals.css`, no asset pipeline, no pattern library, no photo rights. Dark scheme is defined below and stays inside the same hues.

### Direction B — Woven border

Direction A, plus a repeating geometric band derived from textile borders, more gold, and a denser header. The band would be original geometry, not a traced family motif.

Strengths: memorable, and it would connect to the textile sense of the word if the owner confirms that meaning.

Drawbacks: traditional borders are family and regional marks, and many include crosses. A new pattern can still read as costume. Gold fails as text on ivory, so the richer palette fights accessibility. The name meaning is unconfirmed, so the concept is ahead of the business.

Accessibility: every gold line that separates content or shows focus must have a second cue. Pattern tiles must not sit under text. Animation of a "weave" must honour `prefers-reduced-motion`.

Maintenance: high. SVG assets, dark-scheme variants, documentation of what must never be copied from a real cloth, and a review each time a motif is added.

### Direction C — Open room

Large type, stone panels, and photography of people, work, and places. Teal and ink remain the text system. The story is carried by faces and captions.

Strengths: warm and specific when the pictures are true. A consented founder photograph, used with an approved biography, would do more than any pattern.

Drawbacks: no portrait, premises, or client image has been supplied. Stock photography would invent the founder and the clients. Text over images usually fails contrast. This direction also costs the most to keep current.

Accessibility: every photograph needs a real alt text decision (informative or decorative). Text sits on ivory or stone, not on the picture. Captions are required when a person is identifiable.

Maintenance: high. Releases, crops, alt text, and a rule for retiring an image when consent ends.

## 6. Recommendation

Use **Direction A, Clear ground**, now.

Direction B waits on an owner decision about the name. If they confirm the textile meaning, add one original horizontal rule in ink or teal. Do not add a repeating motif in the first redesign. Direction C's photography rules apply later, and only to images the owner provides.

Why this and not a warmer, more decorated system: the business cannot yet show a licence, a founder, or an open service. Decoration would imply a maturity the record does not have. Clear ground leaves room for Amharic and Tigrinya type to become the cultural signature when translations exist. It also keeps gold from becoming a contrast problem.

## 7. Typography

Latin text, including Afaan Oromoo, stays in Noto Sans through `--font-noto-sans`. Amharic and Tigrinya stay in Noto Sans Ethiopic, applied on the element that contains the Ethiopic text. Do not stack the Ethiopic font on the body. The Noto Sans fallback covers the whole Unicode range and will hide Ethiopic glyphs.

Do not add a display face in the first implementation. A second paid Ethiopic family (studios such as TypeHabesha are making them) is a later decision about licence and loading, not part of this approval.

| Use | Specification |
| --- | --- |
| Wordmark | Outlined Noto Sans. "TIBEB" in SemiBold capitals. A gold hairline. "CONSULTANCY" in Medium, tracked to the same width. |
| Page title | Existing `text-title` / `text-title-lg`. One `h1`. |
| Body | Existing `text-body`, line height 1.75, measure no wider than `max-width-content` (48rem). |
| Meta and labels | Existing `text-meta`. |
| Ethiopic | Same sizes as the surrounding Latin. Do not shrink a translation to fit a lockup. |
| Numerals | Lining figures in the text face. No faux-small-caps made by scaling capitals. |

The Ge'ez spelling is not part of the wordmark until the owner confirms the letters. Do not set ጥበብ in the logo on the assumption that the dictionary headword is the brand.

## 8. Logo, icon, pattern

The owner asked on 10 October 2026 for a branded logo. The mark that shipped is a Latin wordmark: TIBEB in Noto Sans SemiBold, a gold hairline, and CONSULTANCY tracked to the same width. It is drawn as letter outlines so the header does not depend on a live font. Ge'ez spelling is still unconfirmed, so it is not in the lockup. There is no crest, flag, or monogram.

The browser icon is the same word, TIBEB, with the gold hairline on a seal square. The earlier single-letter tile is retired.

Approved direction for the logo:

- A wordmark, not a crest.
- Clear space equal to the height of the "T" on all sides.
- Minimum size: the word "TIBEB" no smaller than 16px on screen.
- On parchment, the wordmark is seal brown. On the dark page, it is parchment. On the seal control, it is parchment.
- Gold may underline the wordmark only after a designer checks that the link or control does not depend on that line.
- No flag, lion, cross, globe, or generic "T" monogram. The browser icon is the word TIBEB, not a monogram.
- Do not stretch, outline, or set the wordmark in a script face.

Iconography for the interface: 24px line icons, 1.5px stroke, `currentColor`, rounded caps. Use them for navigation and status, with a visible name. Do not commission a set of cultural pictograms.

Pattern and texture: none in Direction A. If a later approval adds one rule, it is a 2px ink or teal line, full width of the content column, used at most once on a page. No tile, no gold weave, no noise texture behind text.

## 9. Imagery and storytelling

Until the owner supplies images, the site uses type and surface only.

When images are supplied:

- The founder appears only in a photograph they provide, with written permission for that crop and that page.
- Do not illustrate the founder. An illustration is still an invented portrait.
- Do not use a client's face without a consent record that names the page and the duration.
- Do not use traditional dress, a flag, or a landmark as a substitute for the person.
- Alt text names what is shown only when the owner has confirmed the facts in the sentence. Otherwise the image is decorative and the adjacent text carries the meaning.
- No text baked into a photograph.

Visual storytelling is the order of the page, not a film treatment:

1. Who the business is, in the approved sentence.
2. What is and is not offered, from the publication gate.
3. How to read the site in each language, including the notice when a translation is missing.
4. A person, only after a biography exists.

## 10. Voice and microcopy

Voice: calm, specific, short. Say what is true and what is still closed. Address the reader as "you" when the sentence is an instruction. Do not say "journey," "empower," "authentic African excellence," or "weaving your future."

The lines below are draft specifications for a later copy pass. They are not approved public copy, and they are not an instruction to paste them onto the preview.

| Situation | Draft line | Do not write |

English is the only language for these drafts. A translator works from owner-approved English, not from this strategy.

## 11. Founder storytelling requirements

No founder is on file. The brand does not invent one.

Before any founder module is designed, the owner supplies, in writing:

- The name they want published, with the spelling they use.
- The role title they want, without a credential they have not documented.
- Whether a photograph may be used, and the file itself.
- Which facts may be said: city or region only if they want it public, languages they personally speak, and any sentence they have actually approved.
- Which facts must stay private.

The page may not infer:

- A birthplace, a migration story, a church or mosque, or a family textile tradition.
- A licence, a degree, a year in practice, or a community award.
- That "Ethiopian-led" means a particular nation of origin, ethnicity, or religion. Ethiopia's peoples and faiths are not one story. The owner tells the one that is theirs, or the site stays with the approved positioning sentence.

If the owner wants no personal story, the brand is complete without one. Clear ground is designed for that outcome.
